    \item Let $P$ be a triangle in a plane with three extreme points $A=[1,1]^\top, B=[2,4]^\top, C=[4,2]^\top$. Let $x=[2, 2]^\top$ be a point in the triangle $P$. Express $x$ as a convex combination of the three extreme points $A,B,C$. You should write $x=\lambda_1 A + \lambda_2 B + \lambda_3 C$, where $\lambda_1+\lambda_2+\lambda_3=1$ and all nonnegative. You need to give the numerical values of $\lambda_1, \lambda_2, \lambda_3$.

    \textbf{Solution:}
    We need to solve for $\lambda_1, \lambda_2, \lambda_3$ in the equation $x = \lambda_1 A + \lambda_2 B + \lambda_3 C$.
    
    Substituting the given values:
    \begin{align*}
    \begin{bmatrix} 2 \\ 2 \end{bmatrix} = \lambda_1 \begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ 1 \end{bmatrix} + \lambda_2 \begin{bmatrix} 2 \\ 4 \end{bmatrix} + \lambda_3 \begin{bmatrix} 4 \\ 2 \end{bmatrix}
    \end{align*}
    
    This gives us the system of three equations:
    \begin{align*}
    \lambda_1 + 2\lambda_2 + 4\lambda_3 &= 2 \\
    \lambda_1 + 4\lambda_2 + 2\lambda_3 &= 2 \\
    \lambda_1 + \lambda_2 + \lambda_3 &= 1
    \end{align*}
    
    Solving this system of linear equations:
    \begin{align*}
    \lambda_1 = \frac{1}{2}, \quad \lambda_2 = \frac{1}{4}, \quad \lambda_3 = \frac{1}{4}
    \end{align*}
    
    Therefore, $x = \frac{1}{2}A + \frac{1}{4}B + \frac{1}{4}C$.

    \item  Remember that a polytope is a bounded polyhedron. Give an example of a polytope in dimension $n$ defined by exactly $2n$ inequalities, such that removing any inequality describing the polytope makes the resulting polyhedron unbounded.  Hint: First think of an example in $\mathbb{R}^2$ and then generalize it.

    \textbf{Solution:}
    Consider the unit hypercube in $\mathbb{R}^n$ defined by the following $2n$ inequalities:
    \begin{align*}
    x_i \geq 0 \quad \text{and} \quad x_i \leq 1 \quad \text{for } i = 1, 2, \ldots, n
    \end{align*}
    
    This polytope is bounded since all coordinates are constrained between 0 and 1. However, removing any single inequality makes the polyhedron unbounded:
    \begin{itemize}
        \item Removing $x_i \geq 0$ allows the $i$-th coordinate to extend to $-\infty$
        \item Removing $x_i \leq 1$ allows the $i$-th coordinate to extend to $+\infty$
    \end{itemize}
    
    Therefore, the unit hypercube provides the desired example of a polytope in dimension $n$ with exactly $2n$ inequalities where removing any inequality results in an unbounded polyhedron.
    
    By the way, an interesting fact is that examples of the above kind do not occur if the number of inequalities is more than $2n$. For example if a polytope is in dimension $n$ and is defined by $2n +1$ inequalities, then there always exists one inequality such that removing this inequality leaves the resulting (possibly larger) polyhedron bounded. I encourage you to experiment in $n =2$ dimension to see this fact!

    \item Consider the following linear program:
\begin{align*}
    \max \quad & x + 2y \\
    \text{s.t.}\quad & x + y \le 4,\\
    & 2x - y \ge -2,\\
    & 2x + y \le 6, \\
    & x \le 0.
\end{align*}
Convert the above LP to a standard form LP.

    \textbf{Solution:}
    To convert to standard form, we introduce the following variable transformations:
    \begin{itemize}
        \item Let $x' = -x$ (since $x \leq 0$, we have $x' \geq 0$)
        \item Let $y = y^+ - y^-$ where $y^+, y^- \geq 0$ (to handle unrestricted $y$)
        \item Add slack variables $s_1, s_2, s_3 \geq 0$
    \end{itemize}
    
    The standard form LP becomes:
    \begin{align*}
    \min \quad & -x' - 2y^+ + 2y^- \\
    \text{s.t.}\quad & -x' + y^+ - y^- + s_1 = 4 \\
    & -2x' - y^+ + y^- - s_2 = -2 \\
    & -2x' + y^+ - y^- + s_3 = 6 \\
    & x', y^+, y^-, s_1, s_2, s_3 \geq 0
    \end{align*}

\item Alexis Cornby makes her living buying and selling corn. On January 1, she has 50 tons of corn and \$1000. On the first day of each month Alexis can buy corn at the following prices per ton: January $\$300$; February, $\$350$; March, $\$400$; April, $\$500$. On the last day of each month, Alexis can sell corn at following prices per ton: January, $\$250$; February, $\$400$; March, $\$350$; April, $\$550$. Alexis stores her corn in a warehouse that can hold at most 100 tons of corn. She must be able to pay cash for all corn at the time of purchase. Write a linear programming model that can be solved to determine how Alexis can maximize her cash on hand at the end of April.

\textbf{Solution:}
Let us define the following variables:
\begin{itemize}
    \item $X_{iB}$ = tons of corn bought in month $i$ (for $i = 1, 2, 3, 4$)
    \item $X_{iS}$ = tons of corn sold in month $i$ (for $i = 1, 2, 3, 4$)
    \item $X_0 = 50$ = initial corn inventory
    \item $P_0 = 1000$ = initial cash
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Objective Function:}
Maximize cash on hand at the end of April:
\begin{align*}
\max \quad & P_0 - 300X_{1B} + 250X_{1S} - 350X_{2B} + 400X_{2S} \\
& - 400X_{3B} + 350X_{3S} - 500X_{4B} + 550X_{4S}
\end{align*}

\textbf{Constraints:}

1. Inventory constraints (non-negative inventory):
\begin{align*}
X_0 + X_{1B} - X_{1S} &\geq 0 \\
X_0 + X_{1B} - X_{1S} + X_{2B} - X_{2S} &\geq 0 \\
X_0 + X_{1B} - X_{1S} + X_{2B} - X_{2S} + X_{3B} - X_{3S} &\geq 0 \\
X_0 + X_{1B} - X_{1S} + X_{2B} - X_{2S} + X_{3B} - X_{3S} + X_{4B} - X_{4S} &\geq 0
\end{align*}

2. Cash constraints (non-negative cash):
\begin{align*}
P_0 - 300X_{1B} &\geq 0 \\
P_0 - 300X_{1B} + 250X_{1S} - 350X_{2B} &\geq 0 \\
P_0 - 300X_{1B} + 250X_{1S} - 350X_{2B} + 400X_{2S} - 400X_{3B} &\geq 0 \\
P_0 - 300X_{1B} + 250X_{1S} - 350X_{2B} + 400X_{2S} - 400X_{3B} + 350X_{3S} - 500X_{4B} &\geq 0
\end{align*}

3. Storage capacity constraints (at most 100 tons):
\begin{align*}
X_0 + X_{1B} - X_{1S} &\leq 100 \\
X_0 + X_{1B} - X_{1S} + X_{2B} - X_{2S} &\leq 100 \\
X_0 + X_{1B} - X_{1S} + X_{2B} - X_{2S} + X_{3B} - X_{3S} &\leq 100 \\
X_0 + X_{1B} - X_{1S} + X_{2B} - X_{2S} + X_{3B} - X_{3S} + X_{4B} - X_{4S} &\leq 100
\end{align*}

4. Non-negativity constraints:
\begin{align*}
X_{1B}, X_{2B}, X_{3B}, X_{4B}, X_{1S}, X_{2S}, X_{3S}, X_{4S} \geq 0
\end{align*} 

\end{enumerate}
